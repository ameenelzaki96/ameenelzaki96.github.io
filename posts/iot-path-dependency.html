The sticky reality of market dominance in telematics

Into the broad world of IoT · Part 3·5 min read·Industry

In the telematics market, the better technology doesn't always win. Often the earlier technology does. Economists call this path dependency: choices made in the past lock a market onto a particular trajectory. The economist W. Brian Arthur showed how a small, seemingly trivial early lead can permanently shape who ends up with the market.

The QWERTY effect

The QWERTY keyboard survives even though it was never the most efficient layout, because everyone already learned it. Legacy telematics providers benefit from the same historical momentum. Once a fleet is wired into an ecosystem, the switching cost of ripping out hardware and retraining staff holds it in place. History becomes a moat for the incumbents.

The hidden cost: cognitive load

The most overlooked switching cost isn't hardware. It's habit. Organizations build workflows around a particular interface, and the cost of unlearning that interface is often higher than the subscription fee of a better product. That psychological lock-in means even weaker platforms keep their market share, simply because they are the path of least resistance.

Sometimes the switching cost is the strongest feature a product has.

The path-breaker: usage-based insurance

I think 2026's shift toward usage-based insurance (UBI) could be the event that breaks this pattern. When insurance premiums depend on driving data, poor data starts to carry a real financial cost. A tracker that only shows dots on a map is no longer enough. The data has to be accurate and complete enough to price risk.

That forces a shakeout. Providers without strong data and AI capabilities are likely to be absorbed or pushed out, and the industry may consolidate around a few players who can turn raw tracking into high-value risk scoring.

What this means for operators

  • Data quality becomes a commercial asset. Device uptime, accurate trip distances and validated odometers start to affect pricing directly.
  • Lock-in cuts both ways. Choose a platform for where the market is heading, not only for what's familiar.
  • Analytics is the differentiator. The winners will be the companies that can answer "what does this data mean for cost and risk?", not just "where is the truck?"
