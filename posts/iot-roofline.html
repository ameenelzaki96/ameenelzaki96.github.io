The Roofline: why telematics isn't pure SaaS

Into the broad world of IoT · Part 2·5 min read·Industry

Every tech business wants scalability, where adding a customer is as simple as clicking "Create account". In industrial IoT and telematics, that picture runs into a roofline: a physical limit where the high margins of software meet the reality of field operations.

Zone A: "The Inferno"

Before a single byte reaches a dashboard, something physical has to happen. Hardware gets shipped, technicians get scheduled, and wires get spliced into a vehicle. This zone has low margins and high intensity. Nothing about it scales the way software does.

If you don't master technician scheduling efficiency and hardware reliability, you never leave it.

Zone B: "The Heaven"

Only once the hardware is installed and data is flowing reliably do you enter the SaaS zone, with recurring revenue, near-zero marginal cost and the dashboards everyone demos. Before that, it is just a dream.

Unless you master the gatekeeper, the SaaS peak stays out of reach.

Why the top players obsess over field metrics

It's no surprise that the leading telematics companies care so much about First-Time Fix Rate and hardware MTBF (mean time between failures). These aren't side metrics. They decide whether a customer ever reaches the profitable part of the relationship.

This is also why much of my own analysis is about the gatekeeper rather than the dashboard:

  • A survival analysis of devices that found 577 units that never transmitted at all, an installation problem rather than wear-out.
  • A cost map of offline devices, showing what it costs to send someone to each one.
  • A travel-cost map for installers working outside the main cities.

Each one is about getting customers out of the Inferno faster and more cheaply.
